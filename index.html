THE QUESTION BEFORE THE QUESTION

Going through the motions isn't the same as knowing where you're going.

Too many young people are being asked to make important decisions about their future before they've had the opportunity to understand themselves.

What am I good at?What matters to me?What kind of life do I want?Where do I even begin?

Not by telling them who to become.
By helping them discover it.

THE LIFE ON PURPOSE METHOD

Discover. Direct. Deploy.

Purpose isn't something we hand to students. It's something we help them uncover, develop, and put into action.

01

Discover

Who are you?

Explore strengths, interests, values, character and the experiences that have shaped you.

02

Direct

Where are you going?

Connect who you are with what matters to you and possibilities you may not have considered before.

03

Deploy

What will you do with it?

Turn discovery into action through intentional choices, meaningful goals and real-world impact.

THE IMPACT COMPASS

What do you want to impact?

Purpose becomes clearer when we stop asking only “What do I want to be?” and start asking “Who or what do I want to make better?”

The Impact Compass gives students three broad directions to explore. It doesn't tell them where to go. It helps them discover their own direction.

01

People

Teaching. Leading. Creating. Serving. Healing. Building community.

02

Animals

Caring. Protecting. Advocating. Researching. Creating better systems.

03

Planet

Building. Innovating. Conserving. Restoring. Solving challenges.

WHY LIFE ON PURPOSE

The goal isn't just a career.
It's a life with direction.

Life On Purpose isn't simply career readiness. It isn't another motivational talk students forget by Monday. And it isn't about forcing every young person to have their entire future figured out.

WHY THIS EXISTS

It started with one conversation.

Years ago, Godfrey Moye met a 17-year-old young man who had already decided what his future would look like. He told Godfrey that since he was eight, he had known that when he turned 18, he could go on government assistance.

That conversation stayed with him. Not because of the assistance itself, but because a child had spent nearly a decade believing that was the extent of his possibilities.

What happens when young people discover they have choices?

That question eventually became Life On Purpose.
